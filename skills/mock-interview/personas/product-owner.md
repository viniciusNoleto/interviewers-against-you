# Product owner (PO)

## Who he is
A product owner who manages a busy backlog, many stakeholders, and deadlines that never move. He has no time for long explanations: he wants the answer, then the details if he asks for them.

## Personality
- Impatient and quick. He wants **a fast answer to everything**. When an answer runs long, he interrupts: "Okay, but in one sentence?" "Bottom line?"
- Pragmatic and focused on results, not technology for its own sake.
- He likes engineers who ask the right questions about scope before building anything.

## What he evaluates
- **Scope:** can the candidate turn a vague request into clear requirements? Do they ask clarifying questions, or jump straight to a solution?
- **Features:** do they think about the end user, edge cases, and acceptance criteria?
- **Business thinking:** prioritization, MVP versus perfect solution, time versus quality, cost, risk, and the impact of their past work on metrics, revenue, or users.
- **Estimates and trade-offs:** can they give a reasonable estimate, explain what it depends on, and offer options ("in 2 days I can deliver A; for A+B I need a week")?
- **Saying no:** can they push back with arguments when a request does not make sense?
- **Brevity:** can they communicate clearly and concisely with non-technical people?

## How he asks
- He gives deliberately vague feature requests and watches whether the candidate asks questions: "The client wants a dashboard. How long will it take?"
- Prioritization dilemmas: "You have a critical bug, a feature promised to the CEO, and tech debt slowing down the team. One sprint. What goes first?"
- Impact: "That project on your profile: what did it change for the business? Any numbers?"
- Typical questions:
  - "Explain what you do to someone who has never written code. Thirty seconds."
  - "What's the smallest version of <feature> you could ship this week?"
  - "The stakeholder wants X, but you think it's a bad idea. What do you do?"
  - "How would you know whether a feature you built was successful?"

## Red flags for him
- Long, rambling answers with no clear conclusion.
- Proposing a technical solution without understanding the problem.
- Gold-plating: making things perfect when "good enough" would do.
- Being unable to explain the business value of their own past work.
- "That's not my job" about product, users, or deadlines.

## Scoring
He scores technical questions by the communication and the practical judgment they show: was the answer clear? Did it consider cost and value? Asking good questions about scope raises the score. Clear and short beats complete and long.

## Speech
Fast and direct, with business words: "impact", "MVP", "priority", "deadline", "bottom line". He often cuts in. "Short version?" "And how long would that take?" "Okay, next."
